Assumptions & limits

Hypothetical additions use single-family and duplex parcels. Choose actual polygon-centroid separation or shortest border-to-border separation. All-current-license blocking is on by default as a conservative assumption; uncheck it to use only current non-resident permits. Every new parcel is checked against the selected existing blockers and every other new parcel. Existing licenses are retained, so existing-to-existing gaps may be smaller than the slider distance. Neither parcel measure determines compliance with the ordinance, which refers to dwelling units.

Cap-only replaces spacing. The mixed model applies both: it keeps a capped subset of the selected spacing pattern. It does not re-pack newly freed space, so another mixed arrangement could fit more. In both capped models, each neighborhood may have floor(selected cap % × residential GIS plots) residential licensed plots, including existing licenses. Existing licenses above cap are retained without additions. The adjustable cap ranges from 0.5% to 20% in 0.5-point steps.

Density = (current residential licensed plots + hypothetical additions) ÷ residential GIS plots × 100. All current plots appear in green, including nonresidential ones, but only residential plots enter density. This is not the percentage of dwelling units or an estimate of actual Airbnb listings.

Parcel points are a proxy for dwelling-unit locations. No zoning eligibility, incentive status, flood/buildability, demand, owner consent or units-per-building verification is applied. Multifamily buildings and resident-occupied STRs are outside this hypothetical addition model; it cannot establish the legal citywide maximum. Current permit snapshots are not live license verification.

Mixed / multifamily neighborhoods are included by default. Uncheck the top-bar checkbox to focus the map using using the residential-mix screen: at least 70% single-family/duplex among all plots, 80% among residential plots, no more than 15% unknown land use and at least 100 residential plots. This changes the map and list only, not modeled totals. Hypothetical additions still use only single-family and duplex parcels; building height is not known.

City guidance · Ordinance 250965 · Parcel GIS

The cap is applied separately to each neighborhood and rounded down to whole parcels. The overall outcome can be below the selected cap because of rounding and insufficient eligible single-family/duplex parcels. With 100th-percentile size and a 10% cap, 15,366 of 154,751 residential parcels gives 9.93%: neighborhood rounding removes 102.1 parcels from the theoretical target, and CBD Downtown / Crown Center lack seven eligible additions. Existing licenses above a neighborhood cap are retained, so a cap is not an absolute ceiling for those existing cases.
